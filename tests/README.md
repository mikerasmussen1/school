# Baskin School tests

Run the whole suite with `node tests/run-all.js` from the repository root
(`node tests/run-all.js math la` runs only the files whose names contain those
words), or any one file with `node tests/<file>.js`. Each exits non-zero on
failure and prints what it checked. CLAUDE.md lists the gates that must pass
before a push and the rules for writing a test that can actually fail.

Most of these are Word Voyagers, which is where the suite started.
`subject-summary.js` is the first that spans every subject.

| file | what it asserts |
|---|---|
| `today-content.js` | Every step of every day, both grades, all 36 weeks, opens exactly one assignment on the Today tab **and that assignment has real content** — a passage over 80 characters, a prompt over 20, a word list with words, a drill ready to start. 1,512 step-assignments. |
| `find-the-mistake.js` | The daily correction drill: present on every day as step 2, two stages in order, the sentence shown once and never repeated inside the question, options that are real words from the sentence, the prompt matching the kind of fault, four distinct repair options, the step ticking with a score, and a finished drill not surviving a change of day, week or grade. |
| `handwriting.js` | All 72 handwriting assignments carry a title, instructions, a labelled sentence and a working listen button; no week in either grade still tells the child to photograph the page; and no prompt says "this sentence" without supplying one. |
| `no-photo.js` | Neither grade has a photo step or capture buttons, the grown-up marking step survives, steps renumber with no gap, and a child can still finish Thursday. |
| `parent-override.js` | A parent can pass a broken step in either grade on any day; a note is required; the row reads "Passed by a grown-up" rather than "Done" and never claims a score; the day can still be finished; the override is listed on the parent tab with grade, week, day and note, can be cleared with "Fixed", syncs, and is wiped by a reset. |
| `no-daily-cap.js` | Nothing stops a child for having done enough today: the sitting cap is 0, four days closed in one sitting never blocks the fifth, ten consecutive days run without a block, the unfinished-day-behind-you warning still works, and `setPace(2)` can put the brake back. |
| `friday-cold-read.js` | Friday's Speak & Show passage: fifth grade shows the *following* week's passage labelled as a cold read for weeks 1-35 and falls back to its own in week 36; third grade shows the week it has just had, unlabelled; the listen button reads whichever is on screen; and Monday's reading step is untouched in both grades. |
| `thirty-minutes.js` | A close-reading step exists on every day in both grades, each day estimates 22-38 minutes, the five daily purposes are distinct and worded differently for each grade, the panel renders purpose, passage, writing task and self-check across 30 grade-day-weeks, and the markup binds all of them plus the per-step and per-day times. |
| `spelling-exact.js` | Spelling is graded exactly: every one of 936 words in both grades rejects all 14,908 single-letter swaps, deletions and doubled letters, while still accepting capitals and surrounding space; no Word Voyagers drill uses a typo-tolerant type; and the tolerance still works for the drills that want it. |
| `freshness.js` | A stale page reloads itself once at load and never loops; a build that lands mid-session offers a Reload banner instead of reloading under the child; the banner is added once however many times the check runs; the periodic check is installed and no more often than once a minute; offline and unstamped pages do nothing; and `build.json` matches the stamp on all three pages. |
| `redo-day.js` | The parent tab can pick any of the 36 weeks and any of the 5 days in either grade, defaulting to the day on screen; it clears the chosen day and not the one being viewed, leaves the neighbouring day and the other grade alone, needs two presses, and names the lesson (day and week) without any calendar date. |
| `lesson-dates.js` | Word Voyagers is self-paced. Every one of the 360 grade-days shows today's date as Month Day, Year, and none shows its scheduled date in any spelling (unless that happens to be today). Nothing says which lesson he should be on: no "This lesson is for", "school day N of 180", "falls on", year-end date or break schedule. Word Voyagers does not load `la-calendar.js` at all, and names none of its holidays or breaks. The Today title is today's date (Weekday, Month Day, Year), then "Today you will complete Lesson N out of 180 lessons" for the lesson on screen, then the date all 180 would be finished at one lesson every weekday from today; the finish-date rule is checked on fixed dates (weekends skipped, a Saturday starts on Monday) and against an independent count for all 360 grade-days, and "Day N of 5" is gone. |
| `notebook.js` | One notebook entry of five numbered tasks per lesson: Tasks 1–3 (the date, the quote copied word for word with its source, and what it means) sit on the quote card, Tasks 4–5 (the challenge sentence and the critical-thinking answer) sit in the lesson panel, and none is printed twice. Every lesson is named and gives at least two things to look for; Task 1 asks for today's date on all 360 grade-days in each grade's agreed wording: both grades "Weekday, Month, Day, Year" with one fixed example each (3rd grade Friday, January 5, 2018; 5th grade Saturday, March 5, 2016); the parent tab describes the same format per grade; and the parent tab carries specific guidance for all five days, different per grade. (The lesson data still carries a three-item task list ending in the handwriting self-check; the page no longer prints it.) |
| `daily-fact.js` | The end-of-lesson Pokémon fact: one for every day of the year in both grades, tagged with a topic and drawn from the bank that matches that day's lesson purpose, hidden until the day is genuinely complete, never the same fact for both brothers on the same day, and 18 distinct facts per weekday per grade across 36 weeks. Also the daily joke: 60 of them on a 12-week cycle, brothers offset by 30 so they never share one, the punchline hidden until asked for and closing again on the next day; 25 topics in all, no lesson day drawing on fewer than four, and etymology capped at a third of the bank. |
| `challenge.js` | Every notebook challenge names two sentences that appear **verbatim** in that week's passage as **whole sentences** (never starting after "Mr." or "Mrs.", never leaving a quotation mark open), never the same sentence twice, with a valid answer; the decision and the thinking question are both real questions and specific to the passage rather than reusable frames; the grades never share a wording; unwritten weeks return null so the page shows a notice instead of going vague; and the parent tab shows which choice is correct. |
| `quote.js` | The quote ticks from the card it is printed on, on all five days, and opening an assignment does not scroll the page away from it. |
| `item-ids.js` | **Maths.** Which question an attempt actually names. `qid` was `set.id + "-i" + (idx+1)` — the item's **position** — for all 9,300 items, because none has ever carried the stable id the question-types header promises. Insert one question at the top of a set and every attempt already logged shifts by one, silently, and most-missed, dynamic difficulty and the teacher drill-down all read that log. `QTypes.idFor` derives it from content instead. Tested both ways: ids survive insertion and reordering, and *do* change when a question is rewritten — detaching that history rather than misattributing it, which is the better of the two errors. Also that whitespace and case are not a rewrite, exact duplicates stay apart, an authored id always wins, and a copy or a set with no items never invents an occurrence. |
| `math-storage.js` | **Maths.** Where maths' fifteen progress bags are stored. They now go both to the top level of a child's slice (where they have always lived) and to `subjects.math.data` (where every other subject writes), and reads prefer whichever is newer. Asserts every bag survives a round trip, a never-migrated record still reads, a namespace-only record still reads, and — the one that matters — that an older build rewriting the flat keys wins over the stale namespaced copy it carried along. Also that restarting the year clears both copies while keeping the grade placement. And that PROGRESS IS NEVER WRITTEN WITH `setState`: `setState` updates memory, `saveState` updates memory, localStorage and the remote push the teacher's view reads, so a progress bag written the wrong way survives until reload and then vanishes — invisibly, because the child watches it work. Four lesson-step writes shipped that way (autoplay, the step pips, replay and play) while the next/prev arrows beside them saved, so whether a child's place survived depended on which control they pressed. The check scans every `setState` call, scopes to its actual argument (a fixed-width window reported four calls that write no progress at all), skips record loads, and allows a deliberate exception only where a `progress-write-exempt` comment says so — the demo rounds are genuinely not meant to be recorded. And — the gap round-tripping cannot see — that `persist()`, which builds its argument to `packMath` by hand one key at a time, actually writes every bag that is declared. `mathAnchor` shipped declared in state and in `blank()` but absent from `persist()`, so the "this is my day" pin round-tripped perfectly and still reset on every reload. |
| `mission-page.js` | **Maths.** Year One's eight missions render from one template, not eight copies: a single `<main>`, eight manipulative gates that are mutually exclusive, and every one of the ~200 bindings resolving for every mission (an unbound one renders *empty* and only warns). The week heading is recomputed from the bank — the copies had drifted, Mission 07 still claiming three weeks after its bank grew to five — and the per-mission prose, accents and print packs are checked to be genuinely per-mission rather than one shared sentence. Also that a Year Two pilot gets the Year Two page and none of Year One's manipulatives. |
| `unit-complete.js` | **Word Voyagers + Field Notes.** Finishing a unit study is the largest thing that happens in either course and used to pass in silence. Asserts the moment is *true* and *rare*: it fires only when every day (or week) of the unit is genuinely finished, a parent-excused day counts as finished (la-mastery's own rule, so it can never disagree with the child's frontier) while a merely skipped one blocks it, and it fires **once per unit ever** rather than once per dismissal — redoing any step inside a finished unit used to pop the panel again. Also that the panel's numbers are real: the week count, the actual check average, and the unit's own Big Question handed back. Plus that `unitWeeks` parses the en dash in "5–8" — splitting on a hyphen returns one number and every unit looks like a single finished week. |
| `y5-manipulatives.js` | **Maths.** Year Two's eight manipulatives — one per mission, the right one open and no other, none open for a Year One pilot, and every binding resolving so none renders blank. Mostly it checks they are *correct*, because a manipulative is the one screen a child is invited to trust over their own arithmetic: partial products summing to the product, long division reconstructing quotient and remainder with one step per digit, unlike fractions retiling to the true LCD, and the place-value column for 0.7 showing a **7** — it showed a 6, since `0.7/0.1` is `6.999999999999999` and the obvious version floors it. Digits are taken in integer thousandths now. |
| `subject-summary.js` | **All subjects.** Every course that syncs a working record gives Teacher HQ a `summary()`, and its numbers mean what their labels say: Word Voyagers counts a day only by its `:end` key (not the four step-keys inside it) and keeps each grade's work separate; a gap is a day gone past, never a day not yet reached, and never an excused one; Field Notes counts weeks and never days, and a finished week with no check is not scored zero; maths stays thin because its own drill-down sits below. Also that a summary returns `null` rather than an empty row, survives a junk record without throwing, and only emits the four tones Mission Control knows how to draw. |
| `week-glance.js` | **Maths + the review queue.** The teacher's weekly grid, where each colour is a claim: green only on a FIRST-try right (a later miss does not undo it), yellow for wrong-then-fixed, red for tried-and-never-right, and gray for not-answered — with the explicit rule that absence is never red, since pLog ages out at 60 entries per set and "no answer recorded" must not read as a blank the child left. Suggestions come only from red, ranked newest-wrong-first with tries as the tiebreak, and are blocked by a live queue entry, a recent resolution, or a teacher's vouch — "he did this" settles a day's misses out of the mill without repainting the grid. Bundles group by source day and `approveMany` fills what fits, reporting what would not, so approve-all can never silently drop the tail. The queue now carries a week (~20 suggested, 24 cap, yellows filling after the reds) and `serve()` paces it out a few per morning across two channels — warm-up-tier questions go to the sprint, but only when their answer survives its numeric keypad (`sprintFit`), since routing "tenths" to a digits-only box would strand it in the queue forever; everything else, and every unfit entry, goes to the practice block. A wrong answer today does not come back today. The queue itself: approve is idempotent and capped with a stated reason, a right answer retires an item for good while a wrong one counts the try, and retired items are remembered — not deleted — so a fixed question cannot be re-suggested next week. |
| `subject-week-glance.js` | **Word Voyagers + Field Notes.** The same four colours as implemented by the two subjects that describe their own week. LA: a finished day is green, finished-but-stuck yellow, a day PASSED BY red — but Friday unfinished on Wednesday is gray, an excused day is gray with its note (never red — la-calendar's no-schedule-judgement decision holds through the colours too), and the other grade's track is invisible. Science: cells are the child's last eight weeks starting from their OWN first finished week (a mid-year start has no "skipped" weeks before it began), a finished week with no check is green rather than a zero, and a week passed between finished ones is red and named. Both are pinned to emit only the four legal statuses. |
| `school-streak.js` | **All subjects.** The one shared streak, counted in school days rather than per subject — Field Notes runs weekly by design and would break a daily streak every Tuesday for doing exactly what it asks, and Word Voyagers refuses to judge a child against a schedule at all. Asserts the three rules that stop it becoming a stick: weekends and a holiday fortnight are skipped rather than missed, today is never a miss (a streak reading zero at breakfast is a reproach for not having started yet), and any subject counts toward the same run. Also the trap that cannot be caught by reading — day numbers are midnight **UTC** while a school calendar asks `getDay()` in **local** time, so west of Greenwich every weekend lands a day early and the streak breaks each Monday for no reason a parent could explain. On a UTC-4 machine the raw date reports the wrong weekday for *every* day sampled, and dropping the conversion fails 8 of these checks. |
| `math-day.js` | **Maths.** What "today" resolves to and what is in it. The day is the first unfinished set at or after an optional PIN, so a record scattered by testing stops dragging a child back to the earliest hole every morning — and the pin self-advances, is ignored when it dangles, and never rewrites `pChecked` to fake the evidence it skips. Asserts the seven sections keep their order (Challenge before Core, since beating it can excuse Core) and that no two claim the same source, which is what stopped the review queue being served into the sprint and the warm-up block under three different names. The Core skip is gated at 4 Challenge items — measured, not guessed: at least four are authored in 178 of 190 Year One sets (93.7%) and 168 of 175 Year Two sets (96.0%), so the skip is available on all but ~19 days of the two years, and two right answers on a 2-item day can never buy one. Also that the module is actually WIRED: index.html loads it (it did not, first time — `MATH_DAY` undefined and the maths entry blank), loads it after math-sequence, routes the maths subject to the day rather than the mission map, falls back to the map if the module is missing instead of rendering nothing, and produces every one of the 20 `{{ }}` bindings the day view draws — an unbound one renders EMPTY and only warns. Plus the finished-course screen, which every successful child reaches and which first shipped as a blank headline under a "Next up: Sprint" line that was no longer true. And the completion rules a child actually feels: an empty section, a locked Bonus, or an excused Core can never hold a day open, and the header's denominator shrinks when Core is excused so a finished day never reads "5 of 6". |
| `lesson-visuals.js` | **Maths.** Whether the lessons actually DRAW. Every other test here greps `index.html` as text — it can prove a string is in the source and cannot prove a pixel reaches the screen, which is how 158 green checks sat beside six rounds of "still no images". This loads the real curriculum, resolves the real lesson for all 365 days, and runs the view's own arithmetic (extracted to `curriculum/lesson-view.js` so it is callable at all). Asserts the one that caused the trouble — `lstep` defaults to 0, so a lesson whose FIRST step draws nothing opens on an empty pane however good step 2 is, and week 1 was fine only because its authored lessons open on a dot diagram. Also that no lesson is blank throughout, that diagrams keep real size down to a 320px screen, that no week serves one lesson to all five days, and that the cache token is a hash of the curriculum rather than a hand-typed date — a stale one meant browsers kept an old `registry.js` and every fix after it was invisible. |

## Why these live here now

They were written in a scratch directory and lost their history every time the
environment reset. Several bugs in this app were only ever caught because one
of these existed — the drill that scored zero while looking correct, the
assignment panel that showed the wrong block, the reset that did nothing
because it ran after `setState`. They are worth keeping.

## The one thing they cannot do

Everything here is Node driving the component's view-model. Nothing renders a
real browser, so a fault that lives purely in CSS or in the DSL's rendering —
an undefined class, a panel below the fold, a button hidden behind another
element — passes every one of these. Three separate bugs of exactly that kind
reached the running site. Test in a browser as well.
