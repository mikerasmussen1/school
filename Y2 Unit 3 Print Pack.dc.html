− 72

− 216
    0
Round 24 to 20. How many 20s in 93? About four — but 24 × 4 = 96, too big. Try three: 24 × 3 = 72. That fits.

Bring down the 6 to make 216. How many 24s? Round again: 20 into 216 is about ten, but 24 × 10 = 240, too big. Try nine: 24 × 9 = 216 exactly.

The first guess being wrong is normal. Rounding the divisor down makes your guess too big; rounding it up makes it too small. Expect to adjust.

How many digits will the answer have?
4536 ÷ 21
21 × 100 = 2,100 — fits.
21 × 1000 = 21,000 — too big.
So the answer is in the hundreds.
812 ÷ 29
29 × 10 = 290 — fits.
29 × 100 = 2,900 — too big.
So the answer is in the tens.
Why it matters
If you expect a three-digit answer and get two, you dropped a digit somewhere. The estimate catches it before you finish.

Check by Multiplying

Quotient times divisor, plus the remainder, equals what you started with.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
✦
Warm-Up
Multiply back
39 × 24
32 × 21
26 × 34
24 × 46
14 × 7 + 2
6 × 8 + 2
The check, written out
100 ÷ 7 = 14 r 2
──────────────
14 × 7 = 98
98 + 2 = 100 ✓
Quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend. If that does not come out, one of the three numbers is wrong.

The remainder must always be smaller than the divisor. If you get 100 ÷ 7 = 13 r 9, the remainder is bigger than 7, which means 13 was too small a quotient. That rule alone catches most remainder mistakes.
