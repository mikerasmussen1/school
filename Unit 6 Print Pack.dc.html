The four facts
right angle  =  90°
straight line = 180°
full turn    = 360°

Almost every angle question this week is one of these four minus something you already know. Reach for the arithmetic before the protractor.

Challenge
Not graded

Perimeter & Area

Perimeter is the fence. Area is the grass. Knowing one never tells you the other.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
✦
Warm-Up
Read carefully — which one is being asked for?
perimeter of 5 × 3
area of 5 × 3
perimeter of square side 6
area of square side 6
perimeter of 10 × 2
area of 10 × 2
Twenty-four metres of fence, four different fields
1 × 11 = 11
2 × 10 = 20
4 × 8 = 32
6 × 6 = 36
Every field above has exactly 24 metres of fence. The grass runs from 11 to 36 square metres. The square wins — and it always does.
◆
Core
1. Area 48, one side 6. The other side?
2. Perimeter 30, one side 9. The other?
3. Area of a 12 by 7 rectangle
4. Perimeter of the same 12 by 7
5.  An L-shape made of a 6 by 4 block joined to a 3 by 2 block. Draw it, cut it into two rectangles, and find the total area.
★
Challenge
C1. List every rectangle with perimeter 24 and whole-number sides. Which has the biggest area? The smallest?
C2. You have 24 metres of fence and a long wall to build against, so you only need three sides. Now what's the biggest field?

Lesson 1.1 Challenge. C1 · 360° for a square and for any rectangle — four right angles. C2 · 360° altogether, and you're facing exactly where you started. C3 · no. Two right angles already use up all 180°, leaving nothing for the third angle, so the other two sides would never meet.
Lesson 1.2 · Perimeter & Area
Warm-Up 16 · 15 · 24 · 36 · 24 · 20
Core
1. 8   2. 6
3. area 84   4. perimeter 38
5. 24 + 6 = 30 square units
Challenge. C1 · 1×11 (11), 2×10 (20), 3×9 (27), 4×8 (32), 5×7 (35), 6×6 (36). Biggest is the square at 36, smallest is 1×11 at 11. Friday · Angle Guess
Puzzles
on a line beside 47° → 133°
round a point → 130°
perimeter 20, biggest area → 25 (5 × 5)
area 36, smallest perimeter → 24 (6 × 6)
(3,7) to (11,7) → 8
45° eight times → 360°
triangle angles → 180°
regular hexagon → 6
The real question. Squares tile (four 90° corners meet to make 360°) and regular hexagons tile (three 120° corners make 360°). Regular pentagons do not: each corner is 108°, and three of them make 324° — leaving a 36° gap that no number of pentagons can fill. Accept any answer that gets to “the corner angles have to add to exactly 360°.”

Challenge item. Closed field: 5 × 5 = 25. With a free wall: 5 deep by 10 long = 50.
Explanation · 4 points
Score the reasoning, not the writing. Award one point each:
□ 1 · Two rectangles drawn with the same perimeter
□ 2 · Both areas correctly computed and clearly different
□ 3 · Identifies the square as the biggest-area shape
□ 4 · Explains that the fence measures the edge, not the inside
Model answer, kid-sized. “A 2 by 10 and a 6 by 6 both need 24 metres of fence, but one holds 20 square metres and the other holds 36. The fence only measures the edge. Long thin shapes waste fence going out and back, so the square holds the most.”
Total 16 points. 14+ Mission Commander · 11–13 Flight Engineer · 10 or fewer Ground Crew. Item 4 is scored on the estimate-then-measure habit, not on precision; anything within 10° earns the point.
