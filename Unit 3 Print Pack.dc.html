Friday · A Million Dots & Powers
Powers 2³ = 8 · 5² = 25 · 10⁴ = 10,000
3² + 4² = 25 · 2⁵ − 2³ = 24
10⁶ ÷ 10³ = 1,000 · zeros in 10⁸ = 8
Bigger: 3⁴ = 81 beats 4³ = 64
The project. There is no single right answer — the mark is for the method. A typical result: about 25 dots fit comfortably in one square centimetre, so a million needs roughly 40,000 cm², which is a square about 2 metres on a side. Anything between one and four metres, with the working shown, is excellent.

The real question. Powers win when the cards include a small base and a large exponent that isn't 1 — 2 and 9 give 512, far past 2 × 9. Plain multiplication wins when the numbers are close and large: 8 × 9 = 72 beats 8⁹ being unavailable with only those cards, and beats 9² = 81 only if a third card is in play. Accept any pair of examples with the arithmetic done correctly.

Explanation · 4 points
Score the reasoning, not the writing. Award one point each:
□ 1 · Writes an expression with two plausible readings
□ 2 · Computes both answers correctly
□ 3 · Says which one the rules pick
□ 4 · Explains why — a group exists before the loose numbers join it
Model answer, kid-sized. “5 + 3 × 4 could be 32 or 17. The rule says 17, because 3 × 4 is already a group of twelve and the 5 is just sitting next to it. If everyone did it their own way we'd all get different answers off the same line.”
