Two-Step Rules

× 3 then + 2 is not the same machine as + 2 then × 3.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
✦
Warm-Up
Multiply first, every time
2 × 4 + 1
3 × 5 + 2
4 × 6 − 4
5 × 3 + 5
(2 + 4) × 3
2 + 4 × 3
Same two steps, two different machines · put in 4
4  →  × 3  →  12  →  + 2  →  14
Multiply first. The + 2 is added once, at the end.
4  →  + 2  →  6  →  × 3  →  18
Add first and the 2 gets multiplied too — which is where the extra 4 comes from.

Mission 08 · Week 1 · Thursday · Lesson 1.4

Working Backwards

Given the output, undo the machine — last step first.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
✦
Warm-Up
What undoes what
undo + 7
undo × 5
undo − 4
undo ÷ 3
+ 6 gave 13, in?
× 4 gave 28, in?
The machine × 3 + 1 gave out 22. Run it in reverse.
out 22  →  take off the 1  →  21
21  →  divide by 3  →  in 7
Forwards you multiply then add. Backwards you subtract then divide — the opposite operations, in the opposite order. Get the order wrong and 22 becomes 7⅓ instead of 7.
◆
Core
Find the input
1. × 4 − 1 gave 27
2. × 5 + 3 gave 38
3. × 2 + 9 gave 33
4. ÷ 2 + 4 gave 12
5.  A machine × 3 + 5 gave out 5. What went in, and why is that answer surprising?
★
Challenge
C1. Write “undo the machine × 3 + 1” as an equation with a letter in it, then solve it for an output of 22.
C2. Which machines cannot be undone? Try × 0 and see what goes wrong.

Guess My Rule

Five inputs to crack the machine. Choose them well and you'll need two.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
How to play: one person writes a secret rule of the form × ___ + ___ and keeps it covered. The other calls out inputs, one at a time, and records the outputs. Crack the rule in five inputs or fewer and you win the round. Then swap.
The strategy, in two moves
Ask for 1, then 2
The jump between those two outputs is the multiplier, on its own, with the add cancelled out.
Then work out the add
Multiply 1 by what you found. The difference between that and the real output is the add.

Lesson 1.3 · Two-Step Rules
Warm-Up · 9  |  17  |  20  |  20  |  18  |  14
Core · 1 · 31  |  2 · 40  |  3 · 24  |  4 · 43  |  5 · they never agree
Item 5 · the second machine is always 4 more, whatever goes in, because the 2 gets multiplied as well. Accept any answer that shows three inputs and spots the constant gap. C1 · 1, 5, 13, 29. C2 · × 2 − 5 sends 5 to 5 but moves everything else.
Lesson 1.4 · Working Backwards
Warm-Up · − 7  |  ÷ 5  |  + 4  |  × 3  |  7  |  7
Core · 1 · 7  |  2 · 7  |  3 · 12  |  4 · 16  |  5 · 0
Item 5 · zero surprises most students because “nothing went in” still counts as an input. C1 · 3x + 1 = 22, so x = 7. C2 · × 0 destroys the input — every number becomes 0, so there is nothing left to undo.
Friday · Guess My Rule
His own rounds, so no key — but the table should show inputs of 1 and 2 early on, and “inputs used” should fall as the rounds go on. That falling number is the learning.
C1 · zero tells you the least about the multiplier, since × anything leaves it at 0 — though it hands you the add for free. C2 · anything not of the form × ___ + ___, such as “square it,” or a rule that changes between inputs, which is not a machine at all.
