One note before you start
F2 is the interesting one. Almost every fifth grader answers “two,” because doubling one measurement doubles the result and the pattern seems obvious. The answer is eight, and the reason — three dimensions each doubling — is the only thing this mission really has to teach. Everything else is arithmetic he can already do.

layer 1

Each layer is 2 by 3, so each holds 6 unit cubes. Four layers stacked up gives 6 × 4 = 24 cubic units.

That is all the formula says: find the area of one layer, then multiply by how many layers deep it goes. The order does not matter — 2 × 3 × 4 gives 24 whichever way you group it.

Volume 120, base 5 by 6 · how deep?
5 × 6 × h = 120
─────────────
30 × h = 120
h = 120 ÷ 30
h = 4
Find the base area first, then divide the volume by it. One layer holds 30 cubes, and 120 cubes make four such layers.

Check by multiplying back: 5 × 6 × 4 = 120. Every one of these problems hands you that check for free.

Unit Sprint

Convert on sight. Metric first, then the awkward ones.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
How to play: write twenty conversions on cards — ten metric, ten customary. Shuffle. One player reads, the other answers out loud. Time the full deck, then swap and try to beat it. Metric should get fast quickly; customary will not, and that is the lesson.
Why one system is faster than the other
Metric · everything is ten
10 mm = 1 cm
100 cm = 1 m
1000 m = 1 km
1000 g = 1 kg
Customary · all different
12 in = 1 ft
3 ft = 1 yd
16 oz = 1 lb
2 cups = 1 pint
Metric conversions are Mission 01's powers of ten with new labels — the digits move, the point stays. Customary ones have to be learnt individually, which is why they are slower and why the sprint is worth timing.

Items 8, 11, 12 and the Big Question
Quiz 8 · water is volume, measured in cubic units; glass is surface area, measured in square units. 11 · the 2×3×4 needs 52 against 98, so it saves 46 square units — nearly half. 12 · 240 cubic units of water; the glass is 40 for the base plus 96 and 60 for the walls, giving 196 square units. Big Question · full marks for an answer naming all three dimensions doubling — 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 — ideally with a worked example such as 3×3×3 = 27 becoming 6×6×6 = 216. Credit noticing that surface area only quadruples, since a face has two dimensions rather than three. An answer that just states “eight times” with no reason scores half.
