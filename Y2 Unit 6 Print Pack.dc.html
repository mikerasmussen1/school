3/4 of 12 · two steps, in this order
Step 1 · ÷ 4

Step 2 · × 3

Divide by the bottom to find one part. Multiply by the top to find how many of those parts you want.

Doing it in the other order also works — 12 × 3 = 36, then 36 ÷ 4 = 9 — but dividing first keeps the numbers small, which means fewer mistakes.

Tops and Bottoms

Multiplying fractions is the easy one. Straight across, then simplify.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
✦
Warm-Up
1/2 × 1/2
1/2 × 1/3
1/3 × 1/3
1/4 × 1/2
2/3 × 1/2
3/4 × 1/2
Why straight across works, and why no common denominator is needed
2/3 × 3/4
──────────
tops: 2 × 3 = 6
bottoms: 3 × 4 = 12
6/12 = 1/2
Adding needs a common denominator because you are counting pieces, and the pieces must be the same size. Multiplying does not, because you are taking a part of a part — and the answer's pieces are new ones you create by cutting twice.

Two thirds of three quarters is a half. Notice the answer is smaller than both numbers you started with, which is what taking a part of a part always does.

The area picture. Fold one way, fold the other, count the overlap.

2 × 3

2/3 × 3/4 · the overlap is the answer
Gold = 6 of the 12 small boxes
Cut the square into 4 columns and shade 3 of them — that is 3/4. Now cut it into 3 rows and take 2 of them — that is 2/3.

The overlap is 6 boxes out of 12, which is 1/2. The two cuts made 3 × 4 = 12 pieces, and the overlap is 2 × 3 = 6 of them. That is exactly why you multiply tops and multiply bottoms.

Bigger or Smaller

Call it before you compute. The factor tells you which way it goes.
NAME ___________
DATE ___________
✦
Warm-Up
Bigger or smaller than 12?
12 × 1/2
12 × 2
12 × 1
12 × 3/4
12 × 5/4
12 × 1/6
One rule, three cases
Factor under 1
Shrinks
12 × 3/4 = 9. You are taking a part of it.
Factor of exactly 1
Stays
12 × 4/4 = 12. One whole copy, unchanged.
Factor over 1
Grows
12 × 5/4 = 15. More than one copy.
Compare the top to the bottom. Top smaller means the fraction is under one, so the answer shrinks. That check takes a second and catches most errors in this mission.
◆
Core
Predict first, then compute
1.  8 × 3/4
2.  8 × 5/4
3.  20 × 2/5
4.  20 × 7/5
5.  Somebody says 20 × 2/5 = 50. Use the bigger-or-smaller rule to show that is wrong.

C2. 100 × 99/100. Bigger or smaller than 100, and by how much?

Items 11, 12 and the Big Question
11 · half scale gives 6 ft by 4.5 ft; quarter scale gives 3 ft by 2.25 ft. Worth asking whether the area quartered — it did not, it went down to a sixteenth. 12 · 8 batches, since 6 ÷ 3/4 = 8. Big Question · full marks for an answer that reframes the division as “how many halves fit inside six” and notices that small pieces mean lots of them fit. An answer that only says “because you flip it and multiply” scores half: the flip is the shortcut, not the reason, and a student who has only the shortcut has nothing to fall back on when they misremember it.
